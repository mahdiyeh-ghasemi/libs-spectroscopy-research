
# Experimental LIBS and Optical Emission Spectroscopy

## Overview

This repository documents experimental work in Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS) and optical emission spectroscopy, with an emphasis on plasma emission analysis, elemental identification, and self-absorption effects in composite materials.

The work is based on my MSc research in Atomic and Molecular Physics, focusing on the application of laser-induced micro-plasma spectroscopy to the quality control of industrial composites.

## Research Background

My experimental research involved the use of a pulsed Nd:YAG laser to generate micro-plasma on material samples and analyze the resulting optical emission spectra.

The research focused on:

- Laser-induced plasma generation and optical emission.
- Spectral acquisition and analysis.
- Identification of elemental emission lines.
- Investigation of self-absorption effects.
- Spectroscopic analysis of Cu–Al2O3 composite materials.
- Quantitative assessment of material composition.

## Experimental Setup

The experimental system was based on a pulsed Nd:YAG laser operating at:

| Parameter | Experimental specification |
|---|---|
| Laser type | Nd:YAG |
| Wavelength | 1064 nm |
| Pulse duration | 6 ns |
| Pulse energy | Approximately 10–100 mJ |

The optical and detection arrangement involved:

- Pulsed laser source
- Beam splitter
- Focusing lens
- Sample and laser-induced plasma
- Optical fiber
- Spectrometer and detector
- Delay generator
- Oscilloscope
- Computer-based spectral acquisition

## Spectral Analysis

The optical emission spectra were investigated to identify characteristic elemental emission lines.

The analysis included:

- Examination of spectral features.
- Identification of characteristic emission lines using NIST atomic spectral data.
- Investigation of line intensities and spectral behavior.
- Consideration of self-absorption effects in quantitative analysis.

## Self-Absorption and Quantitative Analysis

An important aspect of the research was the investigation of self-absorption in laser-induced plasma emission.

Self-absorption can affect measured line intensities and introduce errors in quantitative spectroscopic analysis.

The research considered self-absorption correction in the context of compositional analysis of industrial composites.

## Material System

The experimental study included Cu–Al2O3 composite materials.

An example of the investigated material system involved an Al2O3 concentration of approximately 1.1 wt%.
